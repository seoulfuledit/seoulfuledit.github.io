Korean Hair Treatment Guide: What to Buy and How to Use

Korean hair treatments for visitors: rinse-off treatments, protein and ceramide formulas, hair packs, how they differ from conditioner, and how to use them.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-22
The short answer

A Korean hair treatment is usually a rinse-off cream or liquid you use after shampoo, much like a richer conditioner or a quick hair mask. Look for proteins like hydrolyzed keratin or lipids like ceramide on the label, leave it on for a few minutes, then rinse.

Hair care is the third most popular category for foreign shoppers at Olive Young, after skincare and makeup, the chain said in August 2026. Foreign shoppers made up about 18% of its hair care sales from January to August.

Korean shelves can be confusing, though. You will see treatments, packs, ampoules and water treatments next to conditioner. Here is what each one is, and how to use it.

What a “treatment” is in Korea

  • Treatment (트리트먼트): usually a rinse-off cream or liquid you use after shampoo. It is often richer than conditioner and stays on for a few minutes.
  • Hair pack or mask (헤어팩): a thicker rinse-off product for a longer weekly session.
  • Water treatment: a thin, liquid rinse-off formula.
  • Conditioner: the lighter everyday step. Some brands sell it separately and suggest pairing it with their treatment.

Protein or ceramide: read the label

  • Protein treatments list ingredients such as hydrolyzed keratin, silk or soy protein. La’dor’s Hydro LPP Treatment lists all three.
  • Ceramide formulas list ceramide NP or similar. Cosmetics Europe’s COSMILE database lists ceramide NP as a hair-conditioning ingredient, one that makes hair easier to comb and gives it a softer, shinier look.
  • Oils show up too: La’dor lists olive and jojoba oils, and Aromatica lists argan oil.

Who should use which

  • Bleached, permed or heat-styled hair: start with a protein treatment.
  • Fine hair that goes flat: try a liquid water treatment, and keep it off your roots.
  • Dry, frizzy ends: a cream treatment or a weekly hair pack.
  • Oily scalp, dry ends: treatment on the lengths only, plus a scalp product. See our Korean scalp care guide.

How to use a rinse-off treatment

  • Shampoo first. Then squeeze out as much water as you can.
  • Apply to the lengths. Work from mid-lengths to ends, where hair is driest.
  • Wait. Aromatica’s directions for its 3-in-1 treatment say 2 to 3 minutes. Hair packs can stay on longer if the label says so.
  • Rinse well. Leftover product can make hair feel heavy.
  • Heavy packs: follow the label for how long and how often.

Where to shop in Seoul

  • Olive Young: 151 of its stores sat in what it calls global tourist districts as of August 2026. Those stores put staff who speak foreign languages on the floor first and post signs in several languages.
  • Brand stores online: La’dor, Moremo and Aromatica each run their own web stores, handy for checking sizes and full ingredient lists before you go.
  • Compare two stores: foreign shoppers surveyed in its tourist-district stores in May 2026 said they visited 2.8 stores on average. Prices and sale items can differ.

Read the back label first

  • Rinse-off or leave-in: the directions tell you which. Leave-in products go on towel-dried hair and stay in.
  • Protein near the top: a sign the formula leans on protein.
  • Fragrance: most of these list it. Skip them if scent bothers you.

Below: three Korean treatments to look for, the Korean words to know, and how to pack them.

Three treatments to look for

  • Protein treatment: La'dor Hydro LPP Treatment. La'dor's Korean store calls it a protein treatment for damaged hair and lists hydrolyzed keratin, soy protein, silk and conchiolin protein, with olive and jojoba oils. The 530 ml bottle is ₩19,500 there.
  • Water treatment: Moremo's line is named for it. The current version on Moremo's Korean store is the Ampoule Water Treatment Miracle 100, sold in 200 ml and 400 ml. It is an option if creams feel heavy on your hair.
  • Cream 3-in-1: Aromatica Rosemary 3-In-1 Treatment, 160 ml. Aromatica describes it as a silicone-free cream for hydration, nourishment and shine, with hydrolyzed corn, soy and wheat proteins plus hyaluronic acid.

Prices change often and sales are frequent, so compare the brand store with Olive Young before you pay.

Korean words on the bottle

  • 트리트먼트 (teu-ri-teu-meon-teu): treatment.
  • 헤어팩 (he-eo-paek): hair pack, a mask.
  • 단백질 (dan-baek-jil): protein.
  • 손상모 (son-sang-mo): damaged hair.
  • 워터 트리트먼트: water treatment, a liquid formula.
  • 씻어내는 / 씻어내지 않는: rinse-off / leave-in.

Before you pack it

  • Big bottles go in checked bags. Korean treatments often come in 500 ml sizes or larger. Liquids and creams count as liquids at airport security.
  • Bag it. Put pump bottles in a zip bag in case the pump opens in your suitcase.
  • Buy the smaller size first. Moremo sells its water treatment in 200 ml as well as 400 ml, so you can see how your hair likes it before you commit to a big bottle.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between hair treatment and conditioner?

In Korea, "treatment" usually means a richer rinse-off product used after shampoo, often with proteins or oils, and often left on for a few minutes. Conditioner is the lighter everyday step. Some brands sell both and suggest using them together, so read the directions on the bottle.

How long do you leave a Korean hair treatment on?

Usually a few minutes. Aromatica, for example, tells you to leave its Rosemary 3-In-1 Treatment on for 2 to 3 minutes after shampooing, then rinse thoroughly. Follow the label, since times vary by product.

What is the best Korean hair treatment for damaged hair?

Start with a protein treatment. La'dor markets its Hydro LPP Treatment for hair that is dry and stressed from perms, color and heat styling, and its ingredient list includes hydrolyzed keratin, silk and soy protein. If your hair is fine and gets weighed down, try a lighter liquid water treatment instead.
