Seoul Travel Tips for Your First Trip: What to Know

Seoul travel tips for a first trip: airport train or bus, the T-money card, which map and taxi apps work, cash or card, plugs, and why you don't tip.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-26
The short answer

Ride the AREX airport train or an airport limousine bus into Seoul, buy a T-money card for subways and buses, and use Naver Map or KakaoMap for directions. Pay by card where you can, but carry some won for T-money top-ups. Korea uses 220V outlets with two round holes, and tipping is not expected, per Visit Seoul and VisitKorea.

A first trip to Seoul goes smoothly once a few small things are sorted: how you get in from the airport, how you pay for the subway, and which apps actually give you directions. None of it is hard. It just works a little differently from home.

Here is what to set up, in the order you will need it.

1. Airport to the city: train or bus

If you land at Incheon Airport, you have two easy options.

  • AREX Express train: runs non-stop between Incheon Airport and Seoul Station in about 43 minutes, Visit Seoul says (as of June 2026). Seats are reserved, and you buy a separate ticket before boarding.
  • AREX All-Stop train: takes about 59 minutes to Seoul Station and stops at Gimpo Airport, Hongik Univ. (Hongdae) and Gongdeok on the way, per Visit Seoul. It works like the subway, with unreserved seats.
  • Airport limousine bus: goes straight to areas such as Myeongdong, Hongdae, Jongno and Gangnam in about 60 to 80 minutes, Visit Seoul says. T-money is accepted, but ticket holders board first.
  • Luggage on the bus: Visit Seoul lists a limit of 2 bags per person, 28 inches or under (as of Nov. 17, 2026. Source: Visit Seoul).

Which one? Take the Express if your hotel is near Seoul Station. Take the All-Stop if you stay in Hongdae. Take the bus if you have big suitcases and your area has a direct route.

2. Get a T-money card

T-money is a tap card for subways, buses and taxis. You can buy it at the airport, subway vending machines and convenience stores, Visit Seoul says.

  • Seoul buses don’t take cash. Have a transport card ready (as of June 2026. Source: Visit Seoul).
  • Tap when you get on and when you get off. On buses, tapping out lets you transfer to another bus or the subway within 30 minutes at a discount, Visit Seoul says (as of June 2026).
  • Bring won to reload it. A physical T-money card can only be topped up with Korean won, the Korea Times reported. VisitKorea says the reload machines are inside subway stations.

3. Use a Korean map app

Google Maps has long been limited in Korea. The government approved Google’s request to export detailed map data in February 2026, with security conditions, but gave no launch date for full navigation, the Korea Times reported.

For now, use what locals use. VisitKorea recommends Naver Map and KakaoMap and says both offer navigation in English. Search the store or hotel name, then pick subway, bus or walking.

4. Taxis: hail one or use an app

Taxis take cash, credit cards or transportation cards, VisitKorea says.

  • k.ride is Kakao Mobility’s version of Kakao T for visitors, available in English, Chinese and Japanese. It accepts credit cards issued overseas, per VisitKorea.
  • Uber also works in Korea, and VisitKorea says the app translates messages to the driver.
  • Late at night, a surcharge usually applies from 22:00 to 04:00 (as of Nov. 17, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).

5. Cash or card

Pay by card where you can, and keep some won as a backup.

  • Foreign cards can fail at some terminals, and many Korean websites and apps reject them, the Korea Times reported in April 2025.
  • ATMs with a Global sign accept international cards, Visit Seoul says. Fees vary by ATM, so check the fee on screen before you confirm.
  • Banks usually give the best exchange rates, per Visit Seoul. Avoid uncertified exchange booths.

6. Plugs and voltage

Korea runs on 220 volts at 60 Hertz, and outlets have two round holes, VisitKorea says (as of Sept. 11, 2026). US plugs need an adapter.

  • Check your chargers. A label that reads “100-240V” means the charger handles 220V with just a plug adapter. If it says only 110-120V, it needs a voltage converter.
  • Forgot one? VisitKorea says hotels may lend or sell adapters, and the airport, convenience stores and duty-free shops sell them.

7. Tipping is not expected

Visit Seoul says tipping is not a Korean custom. Many major hotels and upscale restaurants add a 10 percent service charge instead. In taxis, you pay what the meter says.

Apps to download before you fly

  • Naver Map: routes for subway, bus and walking. VisitKorea calls it the most widely used online map among Koreans.
  • KakaoMap: a second map app in English. Handy when one app finds a place and the other does not.
  • k.ride: Kakao Mobility's taxi app for visitors. VisitKorea says it takes credit cards from overseas.
  • A translation app of your choice, for menus and short questions.

Set up each app and add your card at home, on hotel Wi-Fi, not at the taxi stand.

Your first-day checklist

  • Some cash in won, withdrawn at an ATM with a Global sign. Visit Seoul says these accept international cards.
  • A T-money card, loaded before you head down to the platform.
  • A plug adapter for two round holes, and a quick look at your charger for "100-240V".
  • Your passport in your bag if you plan to shop. Tax refunds need it. Our tax refund guide has the steps.
  • Your hotel address in Korean, saved as a screenshot for taxi drivers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Google Maps work in Seoul?

Only partly, so download Naver Map or KakaoMap as well. Korea approved Google's request to export detailed map data in February 2026, with security conditions, the Korea Times reported, but no launch date for full directions was given. VisitKorea recommends Naver Map and KakaoMap, and says both offer navigation in English.

Do you need cash in Seoul?

A little. Pay by card where you can, but foreign cards can fail at some terminals, and a physical T-money card can only be topped up with Korean won, the Korea Times reported in April 2025. Withdraw some won at an ATM with a Global sign, which Visit Seoul says accepts international cards.

Do you tip in South Korea?

No. Visit Seoul says tipping is not a Korean custom and service staff do not expect it. Many major hotels and upscale restaurants add a 10 percent service charge to the bill instead. VisitKorea says taxis charge by the meter and do not require tips.
