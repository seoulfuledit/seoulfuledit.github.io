Korean Head Spa: What Happens and How to Book in Seoul

What a Korean head spa visit usually includes, from the scalp camera check to the blow-dry, how long it takes, how to book in Seoul, and what to ask first.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-26
The short answer

A Korean head spa is a scalp care session at a salon or spa. It usually starts with a close-up scalp check through a small camera, then cleansing, a scalp mask, a head and neck massage and a blow-dry. The Korea Herald says sessions usually run 60 to 90 minutes. Book ahead, often on Naver Map.

A Korean head spa is an appointment for your scalp. You lie back, someone looks at your scalp up close, cleans it slowly, massages your head and neck, and dries your hair.

Many visitors now plan one into a Seoul trip. The Korea Herald describes head and hair spas as a popular wellness stop for foreign visitors.

What a visit usually includes

Programs differ by shop, but most follow the same order. This is the sequence the Korea Herald describes, with the usual extras.

  • Consultation. A short form or chat about your scalp, hair history and what you want from the session.
  • Scalp check with a camera. The therapist moves a small handheld camera or microscope across your scalp and shows you the close-up on a screen. It is a look at oil, flakes and buildup, not a medical diagnosis.
  • Pre-treatment. A gel or tonic goes on to loosen buildup before washing.
  • Cleansing and scaling. A slow wash, often in a reclining chair, with tools or brushes that lift residue from the scalp.
  • Mask and massage. A scalp mask, then a hand massage of the head that often moves down to the neck and shoulders.
  • Rinse. Some shops finish with a long rinse of warm or herb-infused water poured over the head.
  • Blow-dry. A dry and light styling. Ask if you want it done a certain way.

How long it takes

  • About 60 to 90 minutes, according to the Korea Herald.
  • One hour is common for basic programs. A VisitKorea listing for one head spa, for example, gives 60 minutes for its scrub, serum and massage course.
  • More steps take longer. Shops sell programs by step count or by length. More steps do not always mean better care. Pick the program that fits the time you have.

How to book

  • Naver Map. Search 헤드스파 (head spa) near your hotel, open a shop’s page and tap the booking button. Since June 2026, the Korea Times reports, travelers can verify with a passport instead of a Korean phone number for Naver reservations and payments.
  • Booking apps and travel platforms. Many shops also sell slots through visitor booking sites in English. Read what the program includes before you pay.
  • Messages. Some shops answer through Instagram or KakaoTalk. Send the date, time and program you want.
  • Book ahead. Weekend slots can go first.

On the day

  • Arrive with unwashed hair if you like. Cleansing is part of the session.
  • Skip heavy styling products so the scalp check shows your usual scalp.
  • Bring a hair tie and remove earrings.
  • Say how you feel. If the water is too hot or the massage too strong, tell the therapist. “약하게 해 주세요” means “gently, please.”
  • No tip. Tipping is not customary in Korea, as we explain in our Seoul hair salon guide.

What a head spa is not

A head spa is a beauty and relaxation service. The shop is not a clinic, and the scalp camera does not diagnose anything. If you are worried about hair loss or a scalp condition, see a dermatologist. For products you can keep using at home, see our Korean scalp care guide, and for in-salon conditioning, our Korean hair treatment guide.

Prices change often and differ by shop and program, so check the total on the booking page before you go.

Questions to ask before you book

  • How long is this program? Step counts differ, and so does time.
  • What is the total? Ask whether the price changes with hair length or added treatments.
  • Is a blow-dry included? Some programs end with full styling, some with a quick dry.
  • Chair or bed? Some shops wash you lying back, some seated.
  • Is there English help? Many shops reply through Naver or social media messages.
  • Cancellation rules? Check the notice period before you pay a deposit.

Who should check first

  • Broken skin, sores or a sunburned scalp: wait until it heals.
  • A scalp condition such as psoriasis or seborrheic dermatitis: ask your dermatologist first.
  • Fresh bleach, perm or color: tell the therapist, since products and water heat may sting.
  • Allergies: ask to see the products, and mention fragrance or ingredient allergies.
  • Neck or back pain: ask for a lighter massage.

Words to know

  • 헤드스파 (he-deu-seu-pa): head spa.
  • 두피 케어 (du-pi ke-eo): scalp care.
  • 두피 진단 (du-pi jin-dan): scalp check.
  • 예약 (ye-yak): booking.
  • "약하게 해 주세요." (ya-ka-ge hae ju-se-yo) Gently, please.
  • "총 얼마예요?" (chong eol-ma-ye-yo?) What is the total?

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Korean head spa take?

Usually about an hour to an hour and a half. The Korea Herald reports that sessions usually run 60 to 90 minutes, and longer programs with more steps take more time. Add time for the consultation, drying and paying, and ask the shop for the length of the exact program you book.

Does a Korean head spa help with hair loss?

Treat it as scalp care and relaxation, not a hair-loss treatment. A head spa cleans and massages the scalp, and the shop is not a medical clinic. If you are losing hair, see a board-certified dermatologist. The American Academy of Dermatology says finding the cause comes first.

Do you need to wash your hair before a head spa?

No. Cleansing is part of the session, so you can arrive with unwashed hair. Skip heavy styling products and dry shampoo that day if you can, so the scalp check shows your scalp as it usually is. Ask the shop when you book if you are unsure.
