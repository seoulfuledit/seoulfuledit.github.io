What Is Cica? Centella Asiatica, Explained

What cica means in Korean skincare: the Centella asiatica plant, the ingredient names to find on a label, Korean words for the store, and how to patch test.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-21
The short answer

Cica is the K-beauty nickname for Centella asiatica, a small plant also called gotu kola or tiger grass. On a label, look for Centella Asiatica Extract, Madecassoside, Asiaticoside, Asiatic Acid or Madecassic Acid. Skincare brands use it for a soothing, comfortable feel.

In Korean skincare, “cica” is shorthand for one plant: Centella asiatica. Amorepacific, the Korean beauty group, notes that the plant is often called cica in cosmetics.

You will see the word on toners, creams and masks. Here is what it is, how to find it on a label, and how to try it safely.

The plant

  • What it is: a perennial plant in the Apiaceae family, the plant family of parsley and carrots.
  • Where it grows: India, Pakistan, Madagascar and equatorial Africa, among other places, a 2013 review in Postepy Dermatologii i Alergologii says.
  • Other names: gotu kola, tiger grass and Indian pennywort. In Korean, 병풀 (byeong-pul).
  • What is in it: four main compounds called pentacyclic triterpenes: asiaticoside, madecassoside, asiatic acid and madecassic acid.

How to spot it on an ingredient list

The word “cica” on the front does not tell you what is inside. The ingredient list does. Look for these names:

  • Centella Asiatica Extract: the standard name for the plant extract.
  • Centella Asiatica Leaf Extract: or another plant-part version. Amorepacific lists leaf and stem forms.
  • Madecassoside: one compound purified from the plant. Hwahae notes it often appears next to Centella Asiatica Extract.
  • Asiaticoside, Asiatic Acid, Madecassic Acid: the other three main compounds.
  • On Korean labels: 병풀추출물 (centella extract), 마데카소사이드 (madecassoside), 아시아티코사이드 (asiaticoside).

One product may list several of these. Another may list only one. Both can be called cica.

Cica on the front, centella in the list?

  • Not always the same thing. Some brands use “cica” in a product or blend name. Round Lab, for example, describes its Pine Calming Cica Ampoule as using its own “Pine Cica Activer.” Check the list for the centella names above.
  • Extract or one compound. A product with only Madecassoside uses one purified compound. One with Centella Asiatica Extract uses the plant extract, which holds several compounds, Hwahae explains.
  • Look for specifics. Amorepacific says a product that states how its four main compounds are standardized gives you a better sense of what is inside.

What it does, in cosmetic terms

  • A soothing feel. Skincare brands use centella for a soothing, comfortable feel on sensitive-feeling skin, Hwahae notes.
  • Softer-feeling skin. Formulas often pair it with panthenol and ceramides for a soft, smooth finish.
  • Slow and steady. Amorepacific says changes are gradual, so give a product time.

What cica is not

  • Not medicine. A cica cream is a cosmetic. It makes no promise about any skin condition.
  • Not right for everyone. Some people react to it. Patch test first, as below.
  • Have a skin problem? Ask a dermatologist or a pharmacist, not a shelf label.

Who it suits, per Hwahae

  • Sensitive-feeling skin that wants a gentle, comfortable step.
  • Dry or rough-feeling skin, often in a cream with ceramides.
  • Anyone layering actives like retinol or niacinamide who wants a calm-feeling partner step.

Patch test before you commit

The AAD's method, for any new product:

  • Where: a quarter-sized spot that won't get rubbed or washed, like the inside of your arm or the bend of your elbow.
  • How long: twice a day for 7 to 10 days, using your normal amount.
  • Watch for: red, itchy or swollen skin. If you see it, gently wash the product off and stop using it.
  • Plant allergies: if you react to plants in the Apiaceae family, Amorepacific suggests asking a specialist before you try cica.

Where cica shows up

  • Forms: you will find it in serums, creams and ampoules, Hwahae notes.
  • Common partners: panthenol and ceramides, for a soft, smooth feel.
  • With other actives: it is generally used alongside retinol or niacinamide. Add one new active at a time.
  • Give it time: Amorepacific says any change is gradual, so steady use matters more than a big first impression.

Words to know in a Korean store

  • 시카 (si-ka): cica.
  • 병풀 (byeong-pul): the Korean name for Centella asiatica.
  • 병풀추출물: Centella asiatica extract, as it appears on Korean ingredient lists.
  • 진정 (jin-jeong): soothing. Round Lab's Korean store has a 민감/진정 (sensitive and soothing) category.

Point to the word on your phone if you need help finding a shelf.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is cica in skincare?

Cica is a nickname for Centella asiatica, a plant used across Korean skincare. Brands put its extract or its purified compounds, such as madecassoside, into toners, serums, creams and ampoules for a soothing, comfortable feel. A cica cream is a cosmetic, not a medicine.

Is cica the same as centella asiatica?

Yes. Cica is the cosmetics nickname for Centella asiatica. Madecassoside is different: it is one compound purified from the plant, not the whole extract. A product called cica may use either, so read the ingredient list to see which it contains.

Can you be allergic to centella asiatica?

Yes, though side effects are rare. A 2013 review reports local allergic reactions and burning in some people who used it on skin. Patch test first: the AAD suggests a spot on your inner arm, twice a day for 7 to 10 days. If skin turns red, itchy or swollen, wash it off and stop.
