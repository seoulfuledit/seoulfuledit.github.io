Korean Toner Guide: Toner vs. Essence vs. Skin

What a Korean toner does, why Korean stores call it 스킨 and an emulsion 로션, how toner differs from essence, and how to use toner pads without overdoing it.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-23
The short answer

A Korean toner is a watery step you apply right after cleansing to add hydration before serum and moisturizer. Korean stores often file it under 스킨 (skin), and 로션 (lotion) there usually means a light emulsion, not body lotion. Essence is an optional, slightly richer layer, and toner pads are cotton rounds soaked in toner.

Ask for “toner” in a Seoul shop and you may be pointed to a shelf marked 스킨. Ask for “lotion” and you may get a light emulsion for your face, not a body lotion. The names are the confusing part. The step itself is simple.

What a toner is

Cleveland Clinic describes toner as a water-based liquid you use after cleansing and before moisturizing. It works like a primer for the rest of your routine.

Older Western toners often had a lot of alcohol to remove oil, which could dry and irritate skin, Cleveland Clinic notes. Newer formulas are gentler. In K-beauty, toner is usually a hydrating step, not an astringent, says Round Lab, a Korean skincare brand.

Toner is optional. Cleveland Clinic lists it as a way to step up a routine, not a must.

Three names, decoded

  • 스킨 (seu-kin) or 토너 (to-neo): toner. Sulwhasoo’s Korean store, for one, lists 스킨 as its own skincare category.
  • 로션 (ro-syeon): in Korean facial skincare, usually a light emulsion that goes after toner. Korean retailer Musinsa files it with emulsions and all-in-ones, as 로션/에멀젼/올인원.
  • 에센스 (e-sen-seu): essence, a light hydrating layer between toner and serum.

You may also see 토너/미스트 (toner and mist) as one category, as on Round Lab’s Korean store. A mist is a toner you spray.

Toner vs. essence vs. serum

They go on in that order, thinnest first.

  • Toner: the most watery. Adds a first layer of hydration after cleansing.
  • Essence: a light hydrating layer, and the easiest step to skip. Round Lab says two or three layers of a hydrating toner can cover toner, essence and a light serum.
  • Serum or ampoule: more concentrated, for one goal. See our Korean skincare steps guide for the full order.

How to use a toner

  • Right after cleansing. Swipe it on or spray it, and do not rinse it off, Cleveland Clinic says.
  • Hands or cotton. Cleveland Clinic mentions a cotton ball or round. Round Lab suggests patting a hydrating toner on.
  • Layer if you like. Patting on two or three thin layers is the Round Lab approach for extra hydration.
  • Once or twice a day. That is up to you, Cleveland Clinic says. With strong acids, start every other day.

Pick a toner by its label

Cleveland Clinic suggests matching the ingredients to what you want:

  • Dull-looking skin: AHAs or BHAs, niacinamide, or licorice root or papaya extract.
  • Mature skin: antioxidants such as vitamin C, plus hydrators like hyaluronic acid and glycerin.
  • Oily or acne-prone skin: AHAs and BHAs. A small amount of alcohol can be fine for oily skin, Cleveland Clinic says.
  • Sensitive skin: avoid toners that are heavily fragranced or use a lot of dyes and preservatives, a Cleveland Clinic dermatology resident advises. Our sensitive skin guide shows how to find fragrance on a Korean label.

Toner pads

Toner pads are cotton rounds soaked in toner, usually sold in a jar. Korean retailer Musinsa files them with toners, under 스킨/토너/패드.

  • When: after cleansing, before serum and moisturizer, Round Lab says.
  • How: swipe gently across your face in outward motions, then pat in what is left.
  • As a quick mask: Round Lab suggests leaving a pad on one area for a minute or two.
  • Rough vs. smooth side: some pads have both. The rough side exfoliates, so use it gently.

A pad with acids is an exfoliator. The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) says over-exfoliating can cause redness and irritation, and the more aggressive the method, the less often you should use it.

Try it first

New toner or pad? The AAD suggests testing it on a quarter-sized spot on your inner arm, twice a day for 7 to 10 days. No redness, itching or swelling means you can move it to your face.

Korean words in the toner aisle

  • 스킨 (seu-kin): toner. The everyday Korean word.
  • 토너 (to-neo): toner, the English loanword. Same step.
  • 로션 (ro-syeon): usually a light emulsion that goes after toner, not a body lotion.
  • 에멀젼 (e-meol-jyeon) or 유액 (yu-aek): emulsion.
  • 에센스 (e-sen-seu): essence.
  • 패드 (pae-deu): pad, as in toner pad.
  • 미스트 (mi-seu-teu): mist, a spray toner.

Store signs often pair them: 스킨/토너, 로션/에멀젼, 토너/미스트.

Before you buy toner pads

  • Read the ingredients for acids. AHA (such as glycolic or lactic acid) or BHA (salicylic acid) means the pad exfoliates. Cleveland Clinic says to start those every other day.
  • Check the texture. Some pads have a rough side. Round Lab, for one, says to use the rough side of its 1025 Dokdo Toner Pad on congested areas and the smooth side to hydrate.
  • Sensitive skin? The AAD says sensitive skin may do better with a mild chemical exfoliant and a washcloth than with rubbing. With rosacea, it says to stop exfoliating. See our sensitive skin guide.
  • Skip exfoliating pads on sunburned skin. The AAD says never to exfoliate a sunburn or open cuts.

Two toner routines to copy

  • Morning: cleanser, a hydrating toner patted on by hand, moisturizer, sunscreen.
  • Night: cleanser, toner pad swiped outward, toner patted in for a second layer if your skin feels tight, then moisturizer.

Using a retinoid or benzoyl peroxide? The AAD says exfoliating on top of them can worsen dryness, so keep the exfoliating pad off those nights.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between toner and essence in Korean skincare?

Toner is the first, most watery layer after cleansing. Essence is a light hydrating layer that goes after toner and before serum, and it is the easiest step to skip. Round Lab, a Korean skincare brand, says two or three layers of a hydrating toner can stand in for toner, essence and a light serum.

Is Korean 'skin' the same as toner?

Yes. In Korean stores, 스킨 (seu-kin) is the everyday word for toner. Sulwhasoo's Korean store, for example, lists 스킨 and 로션 as separate categories, and Korean retailer Musinsa groups 스킨, toner and pads together. 로션 in that sense usually means a light emulsion that goes after toner.

How often should you use toner pads?

It depends on what is in the pad. A plain hydrating pad can be part of a daily routine. If the toner has strong acids, Cleveland Clinic suggests starting every other day and building up. The AAD adds that the more aggressive the exfoliation, the less often you should do it.
