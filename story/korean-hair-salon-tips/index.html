Seoul Hair Salon Guide: Booking, Prices, Tipping

Before a Seoul hair salon visit: how to book on Naver Map, how length surcharges work, the perm and color words to know, what to bring, and why you don't tip.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-17
The short answer

Book a Seoul hair salon ahead, usually on Naver Map, and bring three to five reference photos. Expect the posted price to rise with hair length and add-ons, ask for the total before the stylist starts, and skip the tip, because tipping is not customary in Korea.

Korea has a lot of salons. KB Financial Group’s research institute counted more than 110,000 hair salons and barbershops in September 2020, or 21.3 per 10,000 people, the Korea Herald reported. The US figure was about two.

That means choice, and a few local habits worth knowing before you book. Here is how booking, pricing and tipping work.

Three kinds of shop

  • Miyongsil (미용실): the everyday salon for all ages and genders. Go here for perms, color and longer cuts.
  • Ibalso (이발소): the traditional barbershop, mostly for older men, with shaving.
  • Barbershop: short cuts, usually for younger men.

Booking

  • Use Naver. Most salons, including big chains, take reservations mostly through Naver, the Korea Herald says. Weekend walk-ins are often impossible.
  • No Korean phone number? Since June 2026, Naver lets travelers with a foreign passport verify on their phone for reservations, orders and payments, the Korea Times reported.
  • Message first if your hair needs it. If you have curly hair, send a photo through the salon’s social media and ask whether a stylist has experience with it.

How the price works

  • Posted price is a starting point. Price ranges are usually shown outside the shop. Compare a few.
  • Length adds up. Many salons add a length surcharge on top of the base fee for perms and color. Some use bands like jawline, shoulder, collarbone and mid-back. The surcharge can even exceed the main service.
  • Add-ons are optional. Stylists often suggest treatments or products. Declining is fine.
  • Ask for the total. When you get three or more services, salons must disclose each price and the total before they start (reported Apr. 2024; checked Nov. 16, 2026. Source: Korea Herald). For one or two services, ask anyway.

What to bring

  • Reference photos. Three to five images from the front, side and back.
  • A translation app for anything the photos don’t cover.
  • Your passport and a card, the basics visitor guides list.

How long to block out

  • Perm: about 3 hours, Creatrip says.
  • Color without bleach: about 3 hours.
  • Color with bleach: 3 to 5 hours. Plan nothing tight after.
  • Extras: upscale salons often include a head massage, shampoo, snacks and drinks, the Korea Herald notes. Ask what is included when you book.

Tipping

  • Don’t tip. Tipping is not customary in Korea. Mandatory service fees or built-in tips go against Korean rules, while a voluntary tip is legal (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: Korea Times). A warm thank-you is the norm.

Words to show your stylist

  • 커트 (keo-teu): haircut.
  • 펌 (peom): perm. Korean perms default to waves, the Korea Herald notes, so ask for a straight perm by name if that is what you want.
  • 다운펌 (da-un-peom): down perm, a popular option for short hair.
  • 셋팅펌 (set-ting-peom): setting perm, a common Korean perm style.
  • 염색 (yeom-saek): hair color.
  • 탈색 (tal-saek): bleach. It adds hours to the appointment.
  • 클리닉 (keul-li-nik): hair treatment. Fine to decline.
  • 기장 추가 (gi-jang chu-ga): length surcharge.

Phrases for the chair

  • "총 얼마예요?" (chong eol-ma-ye-yo?) What is the total?
  • "기장 추가 있어요?" (gi-jang chu-ga i-sseo-yo?) Is there a length charge?
  • "이 사진처럼 해 주세요." (i sa-jin-cheo-reom hae ju-se-yo) Like this photo, please.
  • "괜찮아요." (gwaen-chan-a-yo) No thanks. Use it for add-ons you don't want.

If a stylist asks whether you have plans after this, the Korea Herald explains, saying yes signals you don't need styling or a blow-dry. Say no if you want it.

Before you sit down

  • Screenshot your photos. Salon Wi-Fi can be slow, so save images to your phone.
  • Tell your hair history. Mention bleach, perms or straightening from the past six months.
  • Space chemical services. Creatrip recommends at least 15 days between a perm and a color.
  • Bring a card and your passport. Visitor guides list both as the basics.
  • Cancel early. Give at least 24 hours' notice to avoid a cancellation fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you tip at hair salons in Korea?

No. Tipping is not customary in Korea, and mandatory service fees or built-in tips go against Korean rules, the Korea Times reports. The posted price is what you pay. A thank-you, 감사합니다 (gam-sa-ham-ni-da), is enough.

How do foreigners book a hair salon in Seoul?

Most salons take bookings through Naver, according to the Korea Herald. Since June 2026, Naver lets travelers verify with a passport instead of a Korean phone number for reservations and payments. You can also message a salon through its social media account and send a reference photo.

Why did my Korean salon price go up?

Usually because of a length surcharge, called 기장 추가 (gi-jang chu-ga). Many salons charge extra for perms and color on longer hair, using bands like jawline, shoulder, collarbone and mid-back. Added treatments raise the bill too. Ask for the total before the service starts.
