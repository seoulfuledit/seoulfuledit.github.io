Korean Skincare Steps, Simply Explained

The real order of Korean skincare steps, from thin to thick, what each step does, and a 3-step minimum you can keep on a busy travel morning.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-22
The short answer

Korean skincare steps go from thinnest to thickest: cleanser, toner, serum, moisturizer, then sunscreen in the morning. The minimum is three steps, cleanser, moisturizer and sunscreen, and you add a toner or serum only when you want more hydration or a specific look.

The “10-step Korean routine” made K-beauty famous. You do not need it. Round Lab, a Korean skincare brand, writes that it was always more of a Western marketing story than a daily Korean habit.

What matters is the order, and knowing what each step is for. Then you can keep three steps or ten.

The one rule: thin to thick

Cleveland Clinic says a routine should go from lightest to heaviest. A watery layer goes under a thick cream, never the other way around. Two exceptions:

  • Sunscreen goes last in the morning, after your other skincare and before makeup. Cleveland Clinic and the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) both put it at the end.
  • Anything a doctor gave you goes on right after cleansing, the AAD says. Their instructions come first.

Each step, in plain terms

  • Oil cleanser (night, optional): dissolves makeup and sunscreen. Round Lab suggests it first at night if you wore either. Not everyone agrees: a Cleveland Clinic dermatologist advises cleansing once.
  • Cleanser: clears away dirt and grime so the next steps can do their job.
  • Toner: in K-beauty, toner is a hydrating step, not an astringent, Round Lab says. Pat it on right after cleansing.
  • Essence: a light hydrating layer, and the easiest step to skip. Round Lab says two or three layers of a hydrating toner can cover toner, essence and a light serum.
  • Serum or ampoule: a more concentrated step for one goal, such as a brighter, more even-looking tone. Cleveland Clinic suggests one serum at a time. Round Lab calls ampoules “rescue products, not daily staples.”
  • Eye cream (optional): goes between serum and moisturizer. Use your ring finger, the AAD says, because it pulls least on thin skin.
  • Moisturizer: seals in hydration and helps keep skin from drying out.
  • Sunscreen (morning): the final skincare step. Cleveland Clinic recommends broad-spectrum SPF 15 or higher.

Why the order matters

A thin serum under a thick cream can reach your skin. Put the cream on first, and the serum has a harder time getting through, Cleveland Clinic explains. That is the whole logic behind thin to thick.

Common mix-ups

  • Moving sunscreen earlier because it feels thinner than your cream. It still goes last in the morning.
  • Stacking serums. Cleveland Clinic suggests one serum at a time.
  • Piling on anti-aging products. The AAD warns this can irritate skin.
  • Oil cleansing every morning. Round Lab suggests the oil step at night, when you have makeup or sunscreen to remove.

Three routines to copy

  • The minimum (3 steps): cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen. Cleveland Clinic calls this the least you want to do.
  • The K-beauty basic (4 steps): cleanser, hydrating toner, moisturizer, sunscreen. This is the four-step base Round Lab describes.
  • The longer night (6 steps): oil cleanser, cleanser, toner, serum or ampoule, eye cream, moisturizer.

On a trip

  • Pack the minimum. Three products cover a travel morning.
  • Shop for one step at a time. Pick the step you want to add, then compare products for that step only.
  • Sheet masks are optional. Save them for a night when you have time.

Add one step at a time

More is not always better. The AAD warns that using too many products may irritate skin, especially more than one anti-aging product.

  • Add one new product and give it time before adding the next.
  • Patch test first. The AAD suggests a quarter-sized spot on the inside of your arm, twice a day for 7 to 10 days. Red, itchy or swollen skin means stop.
  • Keep what you can see working. Drop a step that does nothing you can notice.

Morning vs. night

  • Morning: cleanser, toner (optional), serum (optional), moisturizer, sunscreen.
  • Night: oil cleanser if you wore makeup or sunscreen, cleanser, toner, serum, moisturizer.
  • Retinol: Cleveland Clinic suggests using it at night in place of a serum. If yours is a prescription, follow your doctor, not this list.

Find each step in a Korean store

Korean shops group products by step. These are the category names on Round Lab's Korean store:

  • 클렌징 (keul-len-jing): cleansing.
  • 토너/미스트 (to-neo/mi-seu-teu): toner and mist.
  • 에센스/앰플 (e-sen-seu/aem-peul): essence and ampoule.
  • 로션/크림 (ro-syeon/keu-rim): lotion and cream.
  • 선 케어 (seon ke-eo): sun care.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the correct order for Korean skincare?

Go from thinnest to thickest. Cleanse, then toner, then serum or ampoule, then moisturizer. In the morning, sunscreen goes last, before makeup, even if it is thinner than your cream. If a doctor gave you a treatment, apply it right after cleansing, the AAD says.

Do you really need 10 steps in a Korean skincare routine?

No. Cleveland Clinic says the minimum is a cleanser, a moisturizer and sunscreen. Even Round Lab, a Korean skincare brand, calls the 10-step routine more of a Western marketing story than a daily Korean habit. Start small and add one step at a time.

Is toner necessary in a Korean skincare routine?

No, it is optional. Cleveland Clinic lists toner as an optional step. In K-beauty, toner is usually a hydrating layer rather than an astringent, Round Lab explains. If your skin feels tight after cleansing, a hydrating toner is an easy first step to add.
