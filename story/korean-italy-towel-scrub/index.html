Italy Towel: How to Use Korea's Body Scrub Mitt

What the Korean Italy towel is, why it is named after Italy, how to use it safely at home, and what to expect from a seshin scrub at a Korean bathhouse.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-21
The short answer

An Italy towel is a coarse Korean scrub mitt, first made in Busan in 1967 from rough rayon fabric imported from Italy. Soak first, scrub body skin with light strokes, moisturize right after, and keep it to about once a week, the limit Seoul bathhouse scrub attendants suggest.

In Korea you will find small, scratchy mitts, usually green, near the soap. That is the Italy towel, or 이태리타월. Koreans use it to scrub dead skin from the body, at home and at the bathhouse.

It looks simple, and it is. The skill is in using it gently.

Why “Italy”?

  • Made in Busan. Busan Ilbo reports that Hanil Textile in Busan’s Choeup-dong made the first Italy towels in 1967.
  • Italian fabric. The company had imported rayon from Italy that turned out too rough to use, so it tried the cloth as a scrub towel.
  • Slow start. Early sales were weak, Busan Ilbo notes, because many people still scrubbed with stones.
  • Now everywhere. It became a household item in Korea and a staple at Korean saunas, Wikipedia notes.

Busan, the bath city

  • Lots of bathhouses. Busan Ilbo, citing the bathhouse magazine Jip-ap Mogyoktang (집앞목욕탕), says Busan once had about 1,500 public baths and still runs about 500, roughly as many as Seoul.
  • A second invention. The same report credits a Busan machine shop with an automatic back scrubber in the early 1980s: a spinning disc fitted with an Italy towel.

How it works

The mitt has no grains or chemicals. It works by friction. Warm water softens the top layer of skin, and the coarse weave lifts it off in gray rolls, which is why the habit goes with a long soak.

How to use it at home

  • Soak first. Wikipedia, citing a Vogue writer, advises several minutes in water before you start. Bathhouse attendants told CNN about 30 minutes.
  • Wet the mitt. Never scrub dry skin.
  • Use short, light strokes. That is the AAD’s advice for any brush or sponge. Pink, not red, is the goal.
  • Work by area. Legs, arms, then back. Skip the face, chest and any thin or broken skin.
  • Rinse, then moisturize. The AAD says to apply moisturizer right after exfoliating, since it can dry skin.
  • Once a week at most. Seoul scrub attendants told CNN more often risks over-exfoliation.

Signs to stop

  • Stinging or bright redness. You are pressing too hard.
  • Raw or shiny patches. Stop and let skin rest.
  • Irritation that lasts. See a dermatologist.

At a bathhouse: the seshin scrub

Many jjimjilbang (찜질방) and neighborhood bathhouses offer a paid body scrub called seshin (세신). CNN Travel describes it like this:

  • Separate areas: men and women bathe separately and are scrubbed by attendants of their own gender.
  • Soak first: about 30 minutes, in water at a temperature you are comfortable with.
  • Bar soap before, not body wash: attendants say liquid wash makes skin too slippery.
  • Fully bare: remove rings, earrings and glasses, and bring a hair tie.
  • About 40 minutes on a plastic table, then a rinse.
  • Scrub first, sauna after, the attendants advise.

Prices are posted at the bath area or counter. Ask for 세신 (se-shin).

Who should skip it or go gently

  • Sensitive, dry or acne-prone skin: the AAD says a washcloth and a mild exfoliator may suit you better than a coarse tool.
  • Darker skin tones or dark spots: the AAD advises avoiding strong mechanical exfoliation.
  • Retinoid, retinol or benzoyl peroxide users: scrubbing on top can add dryness.
  • Cuts, sunburn, fresh shaving or waxing: wait until skin is calm.
  • Any skin condition: ask a dermatologist first.

Buying one in Korea

  • Color: green is the standard. Pink is softer and blue is coarser, according to Wikipedia, citing a Vogue writer. Start soft.
  • Shape: most are two squares sewn into a mitt that fits over your hand.
  • Words to ask for: 이태리타월 (i-tae-ri ta-wol) or 때수건 (ttae-su-geon), scrub towel.
  • Pack it dry. It weighs almost nothing and has no liquid limit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is it called an Italy towel?

Because the fabric came from Italy. Busan Ilbo reports that a Busan textile company, Hanil Textile, made the first ones in 1967 from imported Italian rayon that was too rough for its original use. The mitt itself is Korean.

How often should you use an Italy towel?

About once a week at most. Scrub attendants at Seoul bathhouses told CNN Travel that getting scrubbed more often risks over-exfoliation. If your skin turns red, stings or flakes, take a longer break.

Can you use an Italy towel on your face?

No. It is made for the body, and facial skin is thinner. Keep it off your face, broken skin and sunburn. If you have a skin condition or very sensitive skin, ask a dermatologist before using any scrub mitt.
