Korean Pharmacy Skincare: Know What You're Buying

How to shop a Korean pharmacy as a visitor: cosmetics vs. functional cosmetics vs. medicines, what to ask the pharmacist, and one brightening pick.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-16 / Updated 2026-11-16
The short answer

Most skincare in a Korean pharmacy is cosmetics, and some of it is functional cosmetics that Korea's drug regulator reviews for brightening, wrinkle or UV claims. Ointments and acne medications can be medicines, so ask the pharmacist before you buy them and check FDA rules before you bring any home.

Pharmacies in Myeongdong now sell almost as much skincare as medicine. In Seoul’s Jung District, home to Myeongdong, the number of pharmacies rose 20.9% in a year, to 214 in October 2026, Yonhap reported.

Visitors are the reason. Korea Tourism Data Lab estimates that foreign tourists’ spending at pharmacies jumped 196.1% in the first nine months of 2026. A pharmacist who opened a beauty-focused store in Myeongdong in February told Yonhap that nearly all customers were foreigners.

The catch: one shelf can hold a serum and a medicated ointment side by side. Here is how to tell them apart before you pay.

Three kinds of product on the shelf

  • Cosmetics: everyday skincare. Creams, cleansers, masks. No pharmacist needed.
  • Functional cosmetics (기능성화장품): still cosmetics, with a function defined by Korean law, such as helping brighten skin, improving wrinkles or protecting from UV rays. Each product must pass an evaluation by Korea’s Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) or be reported to it.
  • Medicines: acne medications, ointments and other drugs. They are medicine even when they sit next to the masks.

Why visitors shop here

  • Drug-company brands. Some Korean pharmaceutical companies run their own skincare lines. Dongkook Pharmaceutical, for one, launched Centellian24 in 2015.
  • Advice on the spot. Industry representatives say pharmacy beauty products appeal to shoppers who want specialized ingredients and professional advice.
  • Tax-free shopping. Many beauty pharmacies advertise it to international visitors. Bring your passport.

Where you’ll find beauty pharmacies

  • Myeongdong (Jung District): the biggest jump in Seoul, 20.9% more pharmacies in a year.
  • Hongdae (Mapo District): pharmacy numbers up 10.4%.
  • Seongsu (Seongdong District): up 9.4%.

Many of these stores look like cosmetics boutiques, with skincare up front and the counter at the back. Don’t let the layout decide for you. Check what kind of product you are holding.

Before you pay

  • Read the box, then ask. If you can’t tell whether something is a cosmetic or a medicine, ask. Simple Korean phrases are below.
  • Walk to a second shop. Pharmacies on the same street often carry the same lines, and some compete on price.
  • Mention what you already use. A pharmacist can only help with what they know about you.

What to buy, what to ask about

  • Buy freely: cosmetics and functional cosmetics, like the brightening ampoule below.
  • Ask first: anything labeled as medicine, and any ointment or acne product. The pharmacist decides whether it suits you, not a shopping list.

How to ask a Korean pharmacist

  • "약사님 계세요?" (yak-sa-nim gye-se-yo?) Is the pharmacist here?
  • "이거 화장품이에요, 약이에요?" (i-geo hwa-jang-pum-i-e-yo, yak-i-e-yo?) Is this a cosmetic or a medicine?
  • "기능성화장품이에요?" (gi-neung-seong hwa-jang-pum-i-e-yo?) Is this a functional cosmetic?
  • "택스 리펀 돼요?" (taek-seu ri-peon dwae-yo?) Can I get a tax refund?

If words fail, show a photo of the product on your phone. Tell the pharmacist about allergies and any medicine you already take.

Medicines: what not to do

  • Don't buy medicine from a shopping list. Ointments and acne medications can be drugs. Ask the pharmacist whether one suits you, rather than buying because a video said so.
  • Don't assume the rules match. A Korean Pharmaceutical Association representative noted that some medicines sold without a prescription in Korea may face different rules overseas.
  • Know the US rule. The FDA says that in most circumstances it is illegal for individuals to bring drugs into the US for personal use, because many have not been approved there. Read its personal importation page before you pack any medicine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Korean pharmacy skincare products medicine?

Most are not. Serums, creams and masks on pharmacy shelves are usually cosmetics, and some are functional cosmetics that Korea's MFDS evaluates for brightening, wrinkle or UV claims. Ointments and acne medications can be medicines, so ask the pharmacist before buying them.

What is a functional cosmetic in Korea?

It is a cosmetic with a function defined in Korea's Cosmetics Act, such as helping brighten skin, improving wrinkles or protecting skin from UV rays. Each product must pass an MFDS evaluation or be reported to the MFDS for safety and effectiveness. It is still a cosmetic, not a drug.

Can I bring medicine from a Korean pharmacy back to the US?

In most cases, no. The FDA says it is generally illegal for individuals to import drugs for personal use when they are not FDA-approved, with narrow exceptions. Stick to cosmetics, and read the FDA's personal importation page before packing any medicine.
