How to Choose a Korean Sunscreen: SPF and PA

What SPF50+ and PA++++ mean on a Korean sunscreen, how they compare with US Broad Spectrum labels, and how to pick a texture in Seoul or at home.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-20
The short answer

Read two marks on a Korean sunscreen: SPF, which rates UVB protection, and PA, a four-step UVA grade where PA++++ is the highest. US versions say Broad Spectrum instead of PA, and the formula can differ, so read the box you are actually buying.

Korean sunscreen boxes carry two marks. Most US tubes carry one. Learn what each means and you will pick the right tube in a minute, in Seoul or at home.

What the Korean label tells you

  • SPF: rates protection from UVB rays, says Korea’s Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS). Anything that tests at SPF 50 or higher is labeled “SPF50+” (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: MFDS).
  • PA: the grade for UVA protection. It runs PA+, PA++, PA+++ and PA++++. More plus signs mean higher UVA protection.
  • 기능성화장품 (functional cosmetic): in Korea, sunscreen is a cosmetic with a function defined by law. Each product must pass an MFDS evaluation or be reported to it. The MFDS tells shoppers to look for this mark.

What the US label tells you

  • Broad Spectrum: the product passed an FDA test for both UVA and UVB protection.
  • SPF number: mainly a measure of UVB protection, the FDA says.
  • Water resistant (40 or 80 minutes): how long it stays effective while you swim or sweat (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: FDA). No sunscreen is waterproof.
  • Drug Facts: in the US, sunscreen is regulated as a drug, so active ingredients are listed with percentages.

PA++++ vs. Broad Spectrum

They are different tests. PA is a graded scale built on a UVA method developed in Japan and now widely used in Asian markets, a 2026 review in Cureus notes. Broad Spectrum is pass or fail.

So a Korean tube with PA++++ tells you how high its UVA grade is. A US tube tells you only that it passed.

Pick a texture

  • Cream or lotion: the everyday choice. Some, like the Round Lab tube below, are sold as feeling like a moisturizer.
  • Stick: a solid balm you swipe on. Handy for reapplying on the go.
  • Cushion: sunscreen in a compact with a sponge.
  • Spray or mist: the MFDS says to spray it into your hand first, then apply, so it does not get into your eyes or mouth or get inhaled.
  • Mineral filters: look for zinc oxide in the ingredients. Round Lab, for one, sells a zinc oxide version called Mild-Up in the US.

Before you pay

  • Check both marks. SPF covers UVB and PA covers UVA, so look at both numbers, not just one.
  • Check water resistance if you are heading to the beach or a hike.
  • Check which version you hold. The same product can have a Korean and a US formula.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does PA++++ mean on Korean sunscreen?

PA++++ is the highest of four UVA grades on Korean sunscreen labels. The grades run PA+, PA++, PA+++ and PA++++, and more plus signs mean higher UVA protection, according to Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety. It is separate from SPF, which rates UVB protection.

Is PA++++ the same as broad spectrum?

No. They come from different tests. Broad Spectrum is a US pass or fail label for products that protect against both UVA and UVB, per the FDA. PA grades UVA protection in four steps. Both tell you a sunscreen covers UVA, but you cannot convert one into the other.

Is the US version of a Korean sunscreen the same formula?

Not always. In the US, sunscreen is regulated as a drug, so brands sell US-labeled versions with a Drug Facts box. Round Lab has filed US labels for both SPF 45 and SPF 50 versions of its birch sunscreen. Compare the box in your hand before you rebuy.
