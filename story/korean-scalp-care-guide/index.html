Korean Scalp Care: Scrubs, Tonics and Brushes Visitors Buy

Korean scalp care is a visitor favorite at Olive Young. What scalp scrubs, tonics, ampoules and brushes do, how to use each, and what labels mean.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-19
The short answer

Korean scalp care treats your scalp like skin: a scrub to clear buildup, a leave-on tonic or ampoule for a cool, refreshed feel, and a brush to massage it in. At Olive Young, hair tonics and ampoules are a visitor favorite, with foreign shoppers making up 28% of their sales.

Scalp care is a rising trend among visitors at Olive Young. Foreign shoppers made up 28% of the chain’s sales of hair tonics and ampoules from January to August 2026, Olive Young said, above the roughly 18% they made up across hair care overall.

The draw is the format. Olive Young singled out roll-on and brush-tip scalp products from brands such as LABO-H and Lilyeve, which are easy to use and easy to pack.

What Korean scalp care is

The idea is simple: treat your scalp like the skin on your face. Cleanse it well, clear buildup, and keep it comfortable.

  • Scalp scrub: a rinse-off product with salt or other grains that clears buildup from styling products and oil.
  • Scaling shampoo: a deep-cleansing shampoo, often labeled 스케일링 (scaling).
  • Tonic or ampoule: a leave-on liquid you apply straight to the scalp, often with menthol for a cooling feel.
  • Scalp brush: a handheld brush, usually silicone, to massage shampoo or scrub across the scalp.

Who it suits

  • Oily scalp, frequent styling: a weekly scrub or scaling shampoo.
  • A scalp that feels hot or tight: a cooling leave-on tonic.
  • Long or thick hair: brush-tip ampoules reach the scalp without soaking your hair.
  • Sensitive scalp: go easy on scrubs, and try a product on a small area first.

How to use a scalp scrub

Aromatica’s directions for its Rosemary Scalp Scrub are a good template:

  • Wet hair and scalp well with lukewarm water.
  • Apply section by section, using the pointed tip along your parts.
  • Massage gently with your fingertips.
  • Add a little water, work up a lather, then rinse thoroughly.
  • Two to three times a week, in place of shampoo or before it.

How to use a tonic or ampoule

  • Start with a clean scalp, towel-dried.
  • Apply directly to the scalp, not the lengths. Brush and roll-on tips make this easy.
  • Massage it in. Do not rinse. LABO-H’s directions say to use its ampoule daily or as often as you like.

A word on hair loss

Many Korean scalp products carry hair-loss wording on the box. Treat them as scalp care. If you are losing hair, see a dermatologist.

Where to shop in Seoul

  • Olive Young: 151 of its stores sat in what it calls global tourist districts as of August 2026, with staff who speak foreign languages and signs in several languages.
  • Ask in your language: since June 2026, some Seoul Olive Young stores have an AI shopping assistant that answers by voice in eight languages, including English.
  • Shop more than one store: foreign shoppers surveyed in its tourist-district stores in May 2026 said they visited 2.8 stores on average. Lineups and sales differ by store.

Pack it right

  • Tonics and ampoules are liquids. Small bottles can go in your carry-on liquids bag if they meet the size limit; big bottles go in checked luggage.
  • Lock the tip. LABO-H’s ampoule twists to OPEN and CLOSE. Close it before you pack.

Products to look for

  • Scalp scrub: Aromatica Rosemary Scalp Scrub, 165 g. Aromatica describes it as an Andes lake salt scrub with rosemary and a precision tip that glides along your part to clear old buildup.
  • Brush-tip ampoules: LABO-H and Lilyeve. Olive Young named both brands as drawing attention from visitors with roll-on and brush-type scalp products (CJ, Aug. 2026). LABO-H is an Amorepacific brand.
  • A scalp massage brush: Aromatica suggests pairing its scrub with one to work the salt across your scalp.

Product names and lineups change often in Korea. Show a photo to Olive Young staff if you cannot find the exact one.

What the labels mean

  • 두피 (du-pi): scalp.
  • 스케일링 (seu-ke-il-ling): scaling, a deep-cleansing scalp product.
  • 토닉 / 앰플: tonic / ampoule, both leave-on.
  • 탈모 증상 완화 (tal-mo jeung-sang wan-hwa): easing hair-loss symptoms. Korea's MFDS lists "products aiding in alleviating hair loss symptoms" as a functional cosmetic category. It is a cosmetic label, not a medicine, and not a promise.
  • 쿨링 (ku-ling): cooling, usually from menthol.

When to skip the shelf

  • Sudden or patchy hair loss: see a board-certified dermatologist. The American Academy of Dermatology says finding the cause comes first.
  • A sore, red or flaking scalp: ask a dermatologist before scrubbing. Aromatica's own directions say not to use its scrub on broken or irritated skin.
  • Medicated products: some pharmacy scalp products in Korea are medicines. Ask the pharmacist before buying them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should you use a scalp scrub?

Usually two or three times a week. Aromatica, for example, suggests using its Rosemary Scalp Scrub two to three times a week, either in place of shampoo or before it. Scrubbing every day can be too much for many scalps. Skip it on broken or irritated skin.

Do you rinse out a Korean scalp tonic?

No. Most scalp tonics and ampoules are leave-on products. LABO-H's directions, for example, say to apply the ampoule directly to the scalp, massage it in gently and not rinse. Use it on a clean scalp, usually after washing and towel-drying.

Do Korean scalp products help with hair loss?

Treat them as scalp care, not hair-loss treatment. Some Korean products are sold in a cosmetic category for easing hair-loss symptoms, but they are still cosmetics. If you are losing hair, see a board-certified dermatologist. The American Academy of Dermatology says finding the cause comes first.
