Where to Stay in Seoul: 5 Neighborhoods for a First Trip

Where to stay in Seoul on a first trip: Myeongdong, Hongdae, Insadong, Gangnam and Seongsu compared by subway line, airport link and who each one suits.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-26
The short answer

For a first trip, stay in Myeongdong for central shopping and a direct airport bus, Hongdae for cafes, nightlife and the airport train, or Insadong and Jongno for palaces and traditional streets. Gangnam suits business trips and modern Seoul, and Seongsu suits repeat visitors who want cafes and pop-ups. Pick a hotel near a subway station.

Seoul is big, and where you sleep decides how much of your trip you spend on the subway. The good news: the subway is easy, and every area below has a station close by. The real choice is about the mood you want outside your door and how far you are from the airport.

Here are five neighborhoods first-timers usually weigh, and who each one suits.

The short version

  • Myeongdong: central, busy, an easy all-rounder.
  • Hongdae: young, lively, direct airport train.
  • Insadong and Jongno: palaces, hanok and tea houses.
  • Gangnam: modern, business-friendly, south of the river.
  • Seongsu: cafes and pop-ups in old factory buildings, better for a second trip.

Myeongdong (Jung-gu): the easy, central choice

  • Who it suits: first-timers, shoppers, and anyone who wants to walk out the door into the action.
  • Subway: Line 4, Myeongdong Station, Exits 5 to 10 for the shopping streets, per Visit Seoul.
  • From the airport: limousine bus 6001 serves Myeongdong and Dongdaemun, and by train it is about 1 hour 10 minutes with a transfer to Line 4 at Seoul Station, Visit Seoul says (as of June 2026. Source: Visit Seoul).
  • Know before you book: Visit Seoul says the area fills with tourists during holiday periods. Expect crowds.
  • Plan it: our Myeongdong beauty shopping route shows how to use a half day here.

Hongdae: young, lively, and close to the airport train

  • Who it suits: solo travelers, friends, and anyone who wants cafes, street music and a late night.
  • Subway: Hongik University Station, on Line 2, the Gyeongui-Jungang Line and the Airport Railroad.
  • From the airport: about 1 hour on the AREX All-Stop train, with no transfer, per Visit Seoul. Limousine bus 6002 serves Hongdae and Jongno.
  • What it’s like: Visit Seoul describes cafes “around every corner” and street performance stages. Walk north to Yeonnam-dong for coffee, or south toward Hapjeong for handmade goods.
  • Know before you book: the streets around the station stay busy into the evening. Pick a side street if you sleep lightly.

Insadong and Jongno: palaces and tradition

  • Who it suits: history lovers, couples, and anyone who wants old Seoul within walking distance.
  • Subway: Anguk Station on Line 3, or Jongno 3-ga Station on Lines 1, 3 and 5, per Visit Seoul.
  • From the airport: limousine bus 6011 serves Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung and Changgyeonggung palaces, and 6002 serves Jongno, Visit Seoul says (as of June 2026).
  • What it’s like: Visit Seoul calls Insadong a quaint neighborhood with antique shops, art galleries and traditional tea houses. On Sundays the streets close to traffic and fill with street performers. Nearby Ikseon-dong has hanok built between 1920 and 1930, now home to cafes and restaurants, Visit Seoul says.
  • Know before you book: many hotels and guesthouses here sit in small alleys. Check the walk to Anguk or Jongno 3-ga Station before you book.

Gangnam: modern Seoul, south of the river

  • Who it suits: business travelers, return visitors, and anyone headed to COEX or Garosu-gil.
  • Subway: Gangnam Station is on Line 2 and the Sinbundang Line. Sinsa Station, for Garosu-gil, is on Line 3 and the Sinbundang Line.
  • From the airport: limousine bus 6020 serves the Express Bus Terminal and Gangnam. By train, Visit Seoul estimates about 1 hour 50 minutes with a transfer to Line 2 (as of June 2026).
  • What it’s like: Visit Seoul calls the Gangnam Station area one of Seoul’s busiest transportation hubs, with a nightlife district full of restaurants, bars and clubs just north of the station.
  • Know before you book: the palaces and old markets are north of the Han River, so sightseeing days start with a longer ride.

Seongsu: cafes and pop-ups for a second trip

  • Who it suits: repeat visitors, design and fashion fans, and K-beauty shoppers who like to test before they buy.
  • Subway: Line 2, Seongsu Station. Seoul Forest Station on the Suin-Bundang Line is close by, per Visit Seoul.
  • What it’s like: “once known primarily for factories and small workshops,” Visit Seoul says, now full of cafes, design studios, fashion brands and pop-ups that change often.
  • Know before you book: it sits farther east than the other areas, so trips to the palaces take longer. Check the walk from your hotel to Seongsu Station.
  • Plan it: our Seongsu beauty shopping route lists exits and a walking order.

How to pick the exact spot

  • Walk time to the station matters most. Check it in Naver Map or KakaoMap before you book.
  • Count the lines. A station with two or three lines saves transfers every day.
  • Check the airport bus stop. If you will take a limousine bus, look up where your route stops near the hotel.
  • Light sleeper? Look one or two streets back from the main nightlife strips.

Split your stay

On a longer trip, two bases can save travel time. One option is a few nights north of the river (Myeongdong or Jongno) and a few nights in Gangnam or Seongsu. Hongdae is easy for the last night because the AREX All-Stop train stops there on the way to Incheon Airport, per Visit Seoul.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best area to stay in Seoul for first-timers?

Myeongdong is the easiest pick. It is central, on Subway Line 4, and has a direct airport limousine bus, per Visit Seoul. Choose Hongdae instead if you want nightlife and a direct airport train, or Insadong and Jongno if palaces and traditional streets are your priority.

Is it better to stay in Gangnam or Myeongdong?

Myeongdong is better for most first trips because the palaces, markets and shopping streets north of the Han River are close. Gangnam is better for business trips, COEX and Garosu-gil, but Visit Seoul estimates about 1 hour 50 minutes from Incheon Airport by train with a transfer.

Is Hongdae a good place to stay in Seoul?

Yes, if you like cafes, street music and a late night out. Hongik University Station is on Subway Line 2, the Gyeongui-Jungang Line and the Airport Railroad, so the airport is about an hour away by the All-Stop train, per Visit Seoul. If you sleep lightly, book a street or two back from the busiest blocks.
