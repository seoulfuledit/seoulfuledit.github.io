Seoul Shopping Guide: Where to Buy K-Beauty by Area

A Seoul shopping guide for beauty: what Myeongdong, Hongdae, Seongsu, Gangnam and Insadong are each good for, the subway stops, and tax refund basics.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-25
The short answer

For K-beauty in Seoul, go to Myeongdong to stock up, Seongsu to test products and get skin checked, Hongdae for trendy shops near the airport train, Garosu-gil in Gangnam for brand flagships, and Insadong for traditional gifts. Bring your passport: purchases of ₩15,000 or more can qualify for a tax refund (as of Nov. 17, 2026, per VisitKorea).

Seoul has more beauty stores than most visitors can cover in one trip. The trick is to match each area to what you want: a big haul, a skin check, a brand flagship or a gift. Visit Seoul’s own shopping course sends visitors to Myeongdong for K-beauty and to Insadong for traditional crafts, and the other areas fill the gaps.

Here is what each area is for, how to get there, and what to know before you pay.

Myeongdong: the big haul

  • Good for: stocking up in one walk. Visit Seoul says the area has many Korean cosmetics stores with competitive prices.
  • Getting there: Subway Line 4, Myeongdong Station, Exits 5 to 10, per Visit Seoul.
  • Hours: open daily, but hours vary by store, Visit Seoul says.
  • Expect: crowds, street food stalls and several branches of the same chains. Search each store by name in a map app.
  • Plan it: our Myeongdong beauty shopping route gives a half-day order, from Daiso to Olive Young.

Seongsu: test, compare, get scanned

  • Good for: trying products and booking services such as skin scans and makeup lessons.
  • Getting there: Subway Line 2, Seongsu Station. Olive Young N Seongsu is about 118 m from Exit 4, Visit Seoul says.
  • Big stops: Olive Young N Seongsu, about 4,620 square meters over five floors, which CJ Newsroom called the chain’s largest store in Korea in January 2025. AMORE Seongsu, Amorepacific’s flagship, matches foundation and lipstick to your skin tone and is closed on Mondays, per Visit Seoul.
  • Expect: pop-ups that change from week to week.
  • Plan it: our Seongsu beauty shopping route has exits, hours and a walking order.

Hongdae: trendy and close to the airport train

  • Good for: fashion and cosmetics in one area, with a younger crowd. Visit Seoul describes Hongdae R3 Fashion Street as densely packed with clothing and cosmetic stores.
  • Getting there: Hongik University Station, on Subway Line 2, the Gyeongui-Jungang Line and the Airport Railroad. R3 Fashion Street is about 350 m from Exit 9, Visit Seoul says.
  • Big stop: Olive Young Hongdae Town, which CJ Newsroom lists as one of the brand’s regional flagships.
  • Expect: street performers nearby. Visit Seoul lists a busking street close to Exit 8.

Gangnam (Garosu-gil): flagships and makeup artists

  • Good for: brand flagships and a slower, boutique pace. Visit Seoul says Garosu-gil in Sinsa-dong has a variety of fashion stores, character shops and cafes.
  • Getting there: Sinsa Station, on Subway Line 3 and the Sinbundang Line, Exit 8.
  • Beauty stop: Jung Saem Mool Plops, the flagship of Korean makeup artist Jung Saem Mool, with brand showcases and beauty classes. Visit Seoul lists it at 51-1 Garosu-gil, open daily 11:00 to 21:00, about 638 m from Exit 8.
  • A bit of history: Olive Young opened its first store in Sinsa-dong in 1999, CJ Newsroom says.

Insadong: gifts, not skincare

  • Good for: traditional souvenirs. Visit Seoul calls it one of the best places in Seoul to buy traditional Korean art and souvenirs, with handicraft, pottery and stationery shops.
  • Getting there: Anguk Station on Subway Line 3. Visit Seoul pages give both Exit 5 and Exit 6, so check your map app.
  • Skip it for skincare. Visit Seoul’s Insadong page does not describe it as a cosmetics area. Pair it with Myeongdong instead.

If you only have one day

  1. Morning in Seongsu for scans and testing, especially if you booked a service.
  2. Afternoon in Myeongdong to buy what you liked.
  3. Evening back at the hotel sorting tax refund receipts into one envelope.

Tax refund basics

  • Minimum: ₩15,000 or more per purchase at a store with a Tax Refund sign (as of Nov. 17, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).
  • At the register: an immediate refund works for single payments under ₩1,000,000, and no more than ₩5,000,000 for the whole trip (as of Nov. 17, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).
  • Who qualifies: visitors staying in Korea less than six months. Items must be new and unopened, and you must leave Korea within three months of buying them (as of Nov. 17, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).
  • Passport: bring the original every time you shop.

Rules can change, so check at the counter. Airport steps are in our tax refund guide.

Plan your days

  • Group by line. Hongdae and Seongsu are both on Line 2. Myeongdong is on Line 4. Sinsa and Anguk are both on Line 3.
  • Mind Mondays. AMORE Seongsu closes on Mondays, Visit Seoul says.
  • Go to Insadong on Sunday if you like street performers. Visit Seoul says the streets close to traffic that day.
  • Buy heavy things last. Creams and sheet masks add up. Shop the big stores near the end of your trip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is the best place to shop for skincare in Seoul?

Myeongdong is the easiest single stop. Visit Seoul describes many Korean cosmetics stores there at competitive prices, all near Myeongdong Station on Line 4. For testing and skin checks, Seongsu has Olive Young N Seongsu, which CJ Newsroom called the chain's largest store in Korea in January 2025, and AMORE Seongsu.

Is Myeongdong or Hongdae better for shopping?

Myeongdong is better for a big beauty haul in one place. Hongdae mixes clothing and cosmetic stores with street performances and suits a younger, fashion-first trip. Hongik University Station is on the Airport Railroad, so Hongdae also works on your first or last day.

Do I need my passport to shop in Seoul?

You need it for tax refunds. Stores check your passport before taking the tax off at the register, and purchases need to be ₩15,000 or more (as of Nov. 17, 2026, per VisitKorea). Carry the original, not a photo.
