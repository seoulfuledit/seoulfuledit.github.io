Korea Tax Refund on Beauty Shopping: How It Works

How visitors get tax back on Korean beauty shopping: the ₩15,000 minimum, immediate refunds at the register, airport kiosks and what to bring.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-18
The short answer

In Korea you can get tax back on purchases of ₩15,000 or more at stores with a Tax Refund sign, either taken off at the register or claimed before you fly (as of Nov. 16, 2026, per VisitKorea). Show your passport when you pay, keep items unopened, and get customs approval at the airport if the store didn't refund you.

Korea’s price tags include tax. As a visitor you can get part of it back, and cosmetics and perfume are on the list of eligible goods. Olive Young counts the nationalities of its foreign shoppers using Global Tax Free refund data: 192 countries in 2026, according to its Aug. 31 press release.

There are two ways to get the money. One takes the tax off at the register. The other refunds it later, in the city or at the airport.

Who qualifies

  • Visitors: foreigners staying in Korea for less than six months (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).
  • Overseas Koreans: staying less than three months and living abroad for two years or more, or holding permanent residency elsewhere (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).
  • Your items: new, unopened and unused, taken out of Korea within three months of purchase (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).

The numbers to know

  • Minimum: ₩15,000 per purchase at a participating store (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).
  • Immediate refund at the register: each payment must be under ₩1,000,000, and your total for the trip can’t go over ₩5,000,000 (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).
  • Downtown refund booths: up to ₩6,000,000 per receipt (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).
  • Airport refund counters: no limit on the purchase amount (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).

Rules do change. The immediate refund limits doubled on Jan. 1, 2024, from ₩500,000 per purchase and ₩2,500,000 in total, according to the Korean government’s Korea.net news service. Check the counter if a number looks different.

Option 1: Immediate refund at the store

  • Look for the sign. Stores show “Tax Refund” or “Tax Free” with a refund company’s logo.
  • Hand over your passport before you pay and say you want a tax refund.
  • Pay the lower price. The tax comes off on the spot.
  • Nothing to do at the airport for these purchases. VisitKorea lists departure as the last step.

Option 2: Refund later

  • Get a tax refund receipt. A regular receipt won’t work.
  • Downtown: claim at a city refund booth or kiosk with your passport, receipt and a credit card. You still show the items to customs when you leave.
  • Airport: get the customs stamp, then claim at a counter or kiosk.
  • Mobile: some refund companies let you apply through their app or site after customs approval.

What to bring

  • Passport: the original, every time you shop.
  • Receipts: keep tax refund receipts flat in one envelope.
  • Unopened items: pack refunded items where you can reach them at customs.
  • A credit card: downtown booths ask for one.
  • Extra time: give yourself a buffer at the airport if you still need the customs stamp. Customs comes before bag drop, not after.

At the airport, step by step

Skip this if every purchase was refunded at the register. For the rest, VisitKorea lists this order:

  • Check in first, but don't drop your bag yet. Keep refund receipts and the items with you.
  • Go to customs. Show the items, receipts and passport to get the export approval stamp.
  • Then check your bag at the oversized baggage counter.
  • Claim the refund at a refund counter or kiosk. At Incheon, cash is paid in the duty-free zone after security.

Incheon Terminal 1: the refund counter is near Gate 28 on the 3rd floor. Staffed hours are 07:00 to 22:00, and kiosks run 24 hours (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea tax refund guide). Locations change, so check the signs when you arrive.

How much you actually get back

  • About 5 to 8% of the purchase price, after the refund company's processing fee (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea tax refund guide).
  • Method matters. Refunds to a credit card or mobile payment carry separate fees, so you get a little less (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea tax refund guide).
  • Mailbox refunds take time. If the counter is closed, you fill in the receipt and drop it in the box. The refund reaches your card in 1 to 2 months (as of Nov. 16, 2026. Source: VisitKorea).

Say it at the register

  • "택스 리펀 돼요?" (taek-seu ri-peon dwae-yo?) Can I get a tax refund?
  • "즉시 환급 돼요?" (jeuk-si hwan-geup dwae-yo?) Can you take it off now?
  • "여권 여기 있어요." (yeo-gwon yeo-gi i-sseo-yo.) Here's my passport.

Say it before the cashier rings you up. It is easier than fixing a receipt afterward.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum purchase for a tax refund in Korea?

₩15,000 per purchase at a store that offers tax refunds, according to VisitKorea as of Nov. 16, 2026. The minimum was cut from ₩30,000 to ₩15,000 in 2024. Ask for a tax refund receipt when you pay, because a normal receipt is not enough.

Can I get a tax refund at Olive Young?

Many Olive Young stores in visitor areas handle tax refunds. Olive Young counts its foreign shoppers using Global Tax Free refund data, and its Central Myeongdong Town store gives refund guidance in foreign languages. Look for the Tax Refund sign, bring your passport and ask before you pay.

How much do you get back from a Korea tax refund?

Usually about 5 to 8% of the purchase price after fees, not the full 10% VAT, VisitKorea says (as of Nov. 16, 2026). The exact amount depends on the price, the refund company and how you take the money. Card and mobile refunds carry extra fees.
