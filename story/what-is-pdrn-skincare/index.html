What Is PDRN in Skin Care? Salmon DNA and Plant PDRN

PDRN in Korean skin care, explained: what the label calls it, salmon versus plant sources, how creams differ from clinic injections, and three picks.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-17
The short answer

In Korean skin care, PDRN usually shows up on the ingredient list as sodium DNA, taken from salmon or, in some newer products, made with plant extracts like ginseng. In a toner or cream it works as a skin-conditioning ingredient for hydration and glow, which is different from the PDRN injections done in clinics.

PDRN is on Korean beauty shelves everywhere this year, from ₩1,000 sheet masks at Daiso to brand-name creams. You will also hear it called “salmon DNA.”

The name sounds clinical, and Seoul skin clinics do use PDRN in injections. The bottle on the shelf is a different product. Here is what PDRN means on a cosmetic label, where it comes from, and how to choose.

PDRN in one minute

  • The name: PDRN stands for polydeoxyribonucleotide. It refers to DNA fragments, traditionally purified from salmon or trout.
  • On the label: in cosmetics it usually appears as sodium DNA. Cosmetics Europe’s COSMILE database lists it as a skin-conditioning ingredient.
  • Salmon or plant: some brands use salmon-derived DNA. Others, like VT’s Daiso line, make a version with plant extracts such as ginseng.
  • What to expect: hydration and a glowy look, mostly thanks to the moisturizing ingredients around it.

Salmon PDRN vs. plant PDRN

  • Salmon: Anua says the PDRN in its moisturizing cream is derived from salmon DNA.
  • Plant: VT calls its version VT Panax PDRN. On Daiso Mall, VT lists it as water, wild-cultivated ginseng extract, 1,2-hexanediol and sodium DNA.
  • If you avoid animal ingredients: the word “plant” or “vegan” on the box is your best clue. When the source is not printed, ask the brand.

Why everyone is buying it

  • Low prices: at Daiso, a VT PDRN sheet mask costs ₩1,000. It was the top beauty buy for visitors at Daiso Myeongdong, Daiso data showed in April 2026.
  • Easy formats: toners, masks and creams fit into any routine you already have.
  • The clinic connection: the ingredient is known from skin-booster injections, which gives it a medical-sounding halo. That halo does not transfer to the cream.

Who should try it

  • Try it: if you like hydrating toners and masks and want to see the trend for yourself.
  • Skip the salmon version: if you avoid animal-derived ingredients.
  • Go slow: if your skin reacts to fragrance. Both VT products below contain it.

How to read a PDRN label

  • Look for sodium DNA. That is the usual ingredient-list name. The word PDRN on the front is a marketing name.
  • Check how far down it sits. Ingredients are listed from most to least. In both VT products, sodium DNA sits near the end.
  • Notice tiny numbers. VT prints the amount next to the ingredient: 5 ppb in the toner and 250 ppb in the mask. Parts per billion are very small amounts.
  • Find the source. Anua says salmon. VT says ginseng. If the box says neither, the label alone may not tell you.
  • Read the rest of the list. Glycerin, hyaluronic acid and niacinamide do much of the hydrating work in these formulas.

Creams vs. clinic injections

Two very different things share one name. Korean skin clinics offer PDRN and polynucleotide injections as a medical procedure. A toner or cream is a cosmetic you use at home.

The US rules differ too. Injectable PDRN is not FDA-approved for cosmetic use, Dermatology Times reports. Topical PDRN is sold in the US as a cosmetic.

Our take: buy PDRN skincare for hydration and glow, and judge it the way you would any moisturizer. If you are thinking about a clinic procedure in Seoul, talk to a dermatologist first, ideally one you can follow up with at home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PDRN the same as salmon DNA?

Often, yes, at least on the label. PDRN stands for polydeoxyribonucleotide, DNA fragments traditionally purified from salmon. In cosmetics it usually appears on the ingredient list as sodium DNA. Brands such as Anua say their PDRN comes from salmon, while VT's Daiso line uses a version made with ginseng extract.

Is vegan or plant PDRN real PDRN?

It is a different source with a similar label. VT's Korean label on Daiso Mall breaks its plant PDRN down into water, wild-cultivated ginseng extract, 1,2-hexanediol and sodium DNA. Read the full ingredient list if you avoid animal-derived ingredients, and ask the brand when the source is not printed.

Do PDRN creams work like PDRN injections?

No. A Dermatology Times review notes that injections place PDRN under the skin's surface, while a cream stays mostly on top. Any visible change from a cream is more likely to come from hydrating ingredients in the same formula. Injections are a medical procedure, so ask a dermatologist before considering one.
