Korean Double Cleansing: Oil or Balm, Then Foam

How Korean double cleansing works, who needs a cleansing oil or balm first, how to make it turn milky with water, and when one gentle wash is enough.

By The Seoulful Edit Editors / Published 2026-11-24
The short answer

Korean double cleansing means a cleansing oil or balm first, then a water-based cleanser such as a foam. Cleveland Clinic dermatologists say it helps if you wear makeup or waterproof sunscreen, and most people without makeup do fine with one gentle wash. Massage the oil onto dry skin, add a little water until it turns milky, then rinse.

Double cleansing is the first step of many Korean night routines. It sounds like extra work. On most nights, it is only one more minute at the sink.

It is also optional. Here is who it helps, how to do it so the oil rinses clean, and when one wash is enough.

What double cleansing means

“Double cleansing means cleansing your face twice, first with an oil-based product and then followed by a water-based cleanser,” Cleveland Clinic dermatologist Jane Wu, MD, explains.

  • Step 1, oil or balm: loosens oil-based things on your skin, such as makeup, sunscreen and sebum, Dr. Wu says.
  • Step 2, water-based cleanser: usually a foam. It washes away water-based things such as sweat and dirt.

Who it helps, and who can skip it

  • You wear makeup: Cleveland Clinic dermatologist Kathryn Anderson, MD, suggests double cleansing for people who wear makeup.
  • You wear waterproof makeup or waterproof sunscreen: this is where the oil step helps most, Dr. Wu says.
  • Oily skin: Dr. Wu says an oil cleanser can help remove excess oil as a first step.
  • No makeup: Dr. Anderson does not recommend it. It can overcomplicate your routine and be more drying.
  • Dry or sensitive skin: keep it for nights you really need it. Dermatologists worry more about overwashing than about skipping a step, Dr. Wu says.

Oil or balm?

  • Cleansing oil: a liquid, usually in a pump bottle. Round Lab, for one, sells its 1025 Dokdo Cleansing Oil for all skin types.
  • Cleansing balm: a solid you scoop out, which softens as you massage it in. Banila Co sells its Clean it Zero Original balm in a 180 ml jar and in a travel kit of sixteen 3 ml minis on its official Korean store (checked Nov. 17, 2026). The minis are easy to pack for a trip.

Both do the same job. Pick the texture you like using.

How to emulsify a cleansing oil

Emulsifying means adding water so the oil turns milky and rinses away. Round Lab gives these steps:

  1. Start dry. Use completely dry hands on a completely dry face. Water starts the milky change too early, Round Lab says.
  2. Use one to two pumps.
  3. Massage for 30 to 60 seconds in gentle circles. Spend extra time on the forehead, nose and jawline.
  4. Add a little lukewarm water. Wet your hands and keep massaging. The oil turns from clear or golden to white and milky.
  5. Keep going 15 to 20 seconds.
  6. Rinse until the milkiness is gone. Your skin should feel clean, not tight or slippery.

The second cleanse

  1. Stay damp. Dr. Wu suggests skipping the towel between steps.
  2. Use your fingertips. The AAD says a washcloth or sponge can irritate your skin.
  3. Massage gently. Do not scrub, the AAD says.
  4. Rinse with lukewarm water. Water that is too hot can dry out skin, Dr. Wu says.
  5. Pat dry with a soft towel, then go on with your skincare steps.

Pick the second cleanser by skin type

These are Dr. Wu’s suggestions:

  • Dry, sensitive: a gentle, unscented water-based cleanser. Cream cleansers are less drying than foams.
  • Oily, acne-prone: a water-based foaming cleanser.
  • Combination: a water-based foaming cleanser.
  • Normal: a gentle, unscented cleanser. Cream or foam is up to you.

She also suggests avoiding harsh, very drying or fragranced cleansers for every skin type.

Common mistakes

  • Oil on a wet face. Round Lab says to start dry.
  • Rushing the massage. Give it the full 30 to 60 seconds.
  • Hot water. Use lukewarm.
  • Leaving residue. Round Lab says leftover oil can trap debris. Rinse until no milkiness is left.
  • Oil cleansing every morning. Round Lab calls it a night step.

Morning vs. night

  • Night with makeup or sunscreen: cleansing oil or balm, then a water-based cleanser.
  • Night without makeup: one gentle cleanser is enough, Cleveland Clinic's dermatologist says.
  • Morning: one gentle wash. Round Lab says mornings usually do not need oil cleansing.
  • After a workout: the AAD says to wash as soon as possible after sweating.

If your skin feels tight afterward

  • Cut back. Cleveland Clinic's dermatologist worries more about overwashing than about skipping a step. Overwashing can lead to dryness and irritation, she says.
  • Switch your second step. For dry or sensitive skin, she suggests a gentle, unscented cleanser. Cream cleansers are less drying than foams.
  • Try micellar water. Using micellar water to remove makeup before one water-based cleanser is an alternative to double cleansing, she says.
  • Moisturize. The AAD suggests moisturizer if your skin feels dry or itchy. See our dry skin routine.

Korean words on the shelf

  • 클렌징 오일 (keul-len-jing o-il): cleansing oil.
  • 클렌징 밤 (keul-len-jing bam): cleansing balm.
  • 폼 클렌저 (pom keul-len-jeo): foam cleanser.
  • 클렌징 (keul-len-jing): cleansing, the shelf name for the whole step.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need to double cleanse every day?

No. Double cleanse on nights you wear makeup or sunscreen, and wash once the rest of the time. Cleveland Clinic dermatologists say most people do well with one thorough wash using a gentle cleanser, and that double washing can be more drying. Round Lab, a Korean brand, also calls oil cleansing a nighttime step.

How do you emulsify a cleansing oil?

Add a little water after you massage the oil in. Round Lab says to start with dry hands and a dry face, massage for 30 to 60 seconds, then wet your hands with lukewarm water and keep going. The oil turns white and milky. Massage 15 to 20 seconds more, then rinse until the milkiness is gone.

Is double cleansing OK for acne-prone skin?

It can be, but go slowly. A Cleveland Clinic dermatologist warns that an oil-based cleanser can cause breakouts in acne-prone skin, especially used alone. Round Lab says oil cleansing suits acne-prone skin if you rinse thoroughly. Follow it with a water-based cleanser, and stop if you break out.
